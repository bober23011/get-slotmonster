How the peg pyramid works

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row one peg wider than the row above it. When the ball lands on the top peg, it has a roughly even chance of moving left or right, then repeats that split at every row it touches. Once it reaches the bottom, it falls into one of the pockets, and there is one more pocket than there are rows.

Because every bounce is a 50/50 turn, most balls collect near the centre while only a few reach the outer edges. Statisticians call this a Galton board, and the shape of the results follows a binomial curve. The payout table simply flips that curve around: common middle pockets pay less, while rare edge pockets pay more.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket’s multiplier is shaped by how likely that pocket is, then trimmed slightly to leave a house margin. If you multiply the payout by the chance of reaching it, the total comes in a little under one; that gap is the operator’s edge. In most versions, the return to player sits around 97% to 99%, depending on the studio and the settings chosen on the day.

A useful check: no board size or risk setting changes the long-run return in your favour. These controls mainly shift variance — the size of the swings — rather than improving the average result.

Trusted games use a certified random number generator for each bounce, and provably fair versions publish seed hashes so a completed round can be checked later. What none of them can do is let a player guide the ball where it lands.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On regulated platforms, yes. Each bounce comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you verify afterward that the path was fixed before the ball moved.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge prizes grow while their chance of landing gets smaller. The long-run return stays broadly the same across board sizes.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

None is mathematically better. Low risk keeps the ride smoother, while high risk concentrates value in rare edge hits. Pick the one that matches your bankroll and your comfort with swings.
